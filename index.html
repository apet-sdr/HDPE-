How the numbers are worked out

  • Sizes. Two systems. IPS (iron pipe size) outside diameters run 1/2 in through 63 in. DIPS (ductile iron pipe size) outside diameters run 4 in through 60 in and are larger than IPS for the same nominal size, so the pipe fits ductile iron fittings. Not every DR is made in every size, and availability differs by manufacturer. Confirm with your supplier.
  • DIPS outside diameters follow the ductile iron pipe OD series used by AWWA C906. Check them against your manufacturer's data sheet.
  • Units. Every dimension is shown in decimal inches and millimetres. Fractional inches (marked ≈) are rounded to the nearest 1/64 in for quick reading at the machine. Use the decimal value when it matters.
  • Minimum wall = OD ÷ DR, rounded to 0.001 in.
  • Average wall = minimum wall × 1.06, the middle of the +12% tolerance. Average ID = OD − 2 × average wall.
  • Pressure class = 2 × HDS ÷ (DR − 1) for water at 73°F. HDS is 1000 psi for PE4710 and 800 psi for PE2708 / PE3408. Higher temperatures and other fluids need a derating.
  • Weight uses a density of 0.955 g/cm³ (0.0345 lb/in³) and the average wall. Treat it as approximate.
  • Heat soak = 4.5 min per inch of minimum wall. ASTM F2620 requires it as a minimum for pipe 14 in OD and larger. Under 14 in, the melt bead size governs and the soak value is shown for reference only.
  • Cool time = 11 min per inch of minimum wall, held under fusion pressure, then another 30 min before pulling, pressure testing or rough handling.
  • Fusion force = interfacial pressure × pipe end area, π × (OD − wall) × wall. Your gauge reading is this force divided by the machine's piston area, plus drag pressure.
  • McElroy. These are the ASTM F2620 butt fusion parameters that McElroy fusion procedures are built on. Check them against your McElroy machine's data, its DataLogger settings, and your project's qualified procedure before you fuse.
